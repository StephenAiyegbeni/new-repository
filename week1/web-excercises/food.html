CLASSES OF FOODS

CARBOHYDRATES

Carbohydrates are a macronutrient found in food. They provide your body with energy and helps your body function.

Carbohydrates are central to nutrition and are found in a wide variety of natural and processed foods.

some examples are:
  • Rice
  • Bread
  • Sweet potatoes
  • Cassava
  • Yams

PROTEIN

Protein is an essential macronutrient found in both plants and animals.

Protein is essential for growth and repair of cells and tissues. Here are some examples:
  • Beans
  • Fish
  • Meat
  • Eggs
  • Milk

VITAMINS

Vitamins are organic molecules that are vital to an organism for proper metabolic function.

Vitamins are organic substances that are generally classified as either fat soluble or water soluble.
some examples of vitamin giving foods include:
  • Oranges
  • Apples
  • Blueberries
  • Banana
  • Mangoes

MINERALS

Minerals in food are essential inorganic nutrients that are necessary for various body functions.

The body uses minerals to do many things — from building strong bones to sending nerve impulses. Examples:
  • Cocoa
  • Mushrooms
  • Nuts and seeds
  • Table salt
  • Seafoods

FATS

Fats are a type of nutrient, a major source of energy, and essential for various bodily functions, including building cell membranes and absorbing vitamins.

Fats are complex molecules composed of fatty acids and glycerol, and they are one of the three main macronutrients
  • Greek yoghurt
  • lard
  • margarine
  • Cheese
  • seed oils

DIETARY FIBRE

Dietary fiber or roughage is the portion of plant-derived food that cannot be completely broken down by human digestive enzymes.

Dietary fibre is often referred to as non-starch polysaccharides (NSP) fibre or as AOAC fibre. Examples:
  • Barley
  • Lentils
  • Oatmeal
  • Whole grains
  • Wheat

WATER

water is a crucial nutrient, essential for life and vital for numerous bodily functions, acting as a medium for chemical reactions and contributing to food stability and texture.

water in classes of food include hydration, nutrient absorption, digestion and metabolism and body temperature regulation.

Some examples of water include:
Hard Water:
Water containing high levels of dissolved calcium and magnesium, often leading to soap scum and scale buildup.
Alkaline Water:
Water with a pH level higher than neutral, often marketed for its potential health benefits.
Mineral Water:
Water containing naturally occurring minerals, often sourced from underground springs.
